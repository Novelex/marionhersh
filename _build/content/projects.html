Overview

  1. Survey

    A survey of autistic people received 78 responses from older (50 and above) autistic people. It was analysed to understand more about their living, working and other experiences, their expertise and willingness to use and learn technology, and their support needs. This identified a guide to autistic friendly spaces and services, and support for executive functioning, as the main areas of interest for support.

  2. Specification

    Follow-up research involving focus groups and interviews produced detailed specifications for a website about autistic friendly spaces and services, and a forum for older autistic people to support community.

  3. Next steps

    We also decided to apply for further funding to develop a support system for executive functioning, and work on this is in progress.

Outcome: Calm Spaces website and forum

We chose the name Calm Spaces in line with the preferences and sensory requirements of many (older) autistic people. The website and forum are now fully functional.

  • The website lets older autistic people register and review spaces and services, including online ones aimed at older autistic people. Anyone can read it without registering.
  • The forum supports the development of a community of older autistic people and is open only to older autistic people.
  • The website and forum share a single login.

Visit calmspaces.org

Framework for ICT and older people

The user and technology factors may seem very similar. However, both sets are needed to investigate the match between user requirements and what a particular technology provides.

Recommendations

Older autistic people and ICT

  • Recognise older people, including older autistic people and other minority groups, as a significant and growing group of ICT users, and involve them in ICT design, development and modification.
  • Provide easy to find and use customisation options for turning feedback and notifications on and off, and for changing colour and text size.
  • Provide default large print, calm options.

Services: training and attitudes

  • Provide training developed and taught by (older) autistic people on their experiences and needs to all personnel.
  • Avoid stereotypes of (older) autistic people, and listen to them.

Services: physical access

  • Provide good quality online services in addition to in-person ones where feasible.
  • Provide assistance to get to appointments in large buildings, such as hospitals.

Services: communication

  • Provide options to book appointments using email and online forms.
  • Minimise the personal interaction needed to access services, and avoid chatty or gatekeeping reception desks.
  • Offer information and questions in writing in advance of appointments.
  • Provide options to communicate in writing during appointments.
  • Allow additional time for (older) autistic people to provide information or respond to questions.

Services: sensory issues

  • Provide separate calm waiting areas, and calm spaces to escape to where talking is not permitted.
  • Check in advance whether service users want music, radio and so on off or on during appointments.
  • If there are repairs or other noisy work nearby, arrange a different appointment location or, if this is not possible, check whether the service user would prefer to reschedule.
