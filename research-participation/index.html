Community website and forum

Calm Spaces: a website and forum for older autistic people

Who
Older autistic people, aged 50 and above
What
Add reviews of services and venues, and join the forum

The Calm Spaces website provides reviews of autistic friendly (and not so friendly) services and venues by older autistic people, for older autistic people.

The forum has posts on a wide range of topics and is intended to support the development of a community of older autistic people. It is linked to the website, but it is a space for older autistic people (50 and above) only: only they can post on it and read the posts. The website and forum were developed by a team of three older autistic people, with support from an advisory board and input from the wider older autistic community.

We are still building up content and encouraging older autistic people to register and add posts and reviews. It is still early days for the Calm Spaces community, so please don’t be disappointed that things are a bit sparse. Please also encourage other older autistic people you know to add reviews and posts.

Survey

Designing recommender systems for blind, partially sighted and autistic people

Who
Blind, partially sighted and autistic people (formally or self-diagnosed)
What
An anonymous questionnaire

We are Marion Hersh, a senior lecturer at the University of Glasgow in Scotland, and Barbara Leporini, an associate professor at the University of Pisa, Italy. One of us is blind and one is autistic.

Recommender systems help people choose products and services when there are a lot of options, by providing a list of suggestions. Examples include Netflix’s recommendations for films and TV programmes and Amazon’s for products, based on purchase or viewing history, ratings and search history. However, most have not been designed with the specific needs of blind, partially sighted and autistic people in mind.

This study will investigate autistic, blind and partially sighted people’s experiences of using recommender systems and of any involvement in their design, and gather suggestions for improving the design process so that it involves them and produces recommender systems which meet their needs.

We have ethical approval from the University of Glasgow. You can complete the questionnaire anonymously, and your answers are confidential to the two researchers.

Please contact us if you have questions or would like more information:

  • Dr Marion Hersh · marion.hersh@glasgow.ac.uk
  • Dr Barbara Leporini · barbara.leporini@unipi.it

Thank you very much for considering taking part in our study.

Survey

AI research and disabled, neurodivergent and Deaf researchers and other professionals

Who
Researchers, research students and other professionals in AI who identify as disabled, neurodivergent or Deaf, with or without a diagnosis
What
An online questionnaire about experiences and attitudes

Artificial intelligence is becoming all-pervasive, but there is minimal research on disabled, neurodivergent and Deaf researchers and other professionals in AI. We (two disabled and neurodivergent researchers and one other researcher) are carrying out a survey to fill this gap.

The study has ethical approval from the College of Science and Engineering at the University of Glasgow.
