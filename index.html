Using technology to overcome the disadvantages experienced by disabled and older people

Marion believes in working to make the world a better place for people, animals and the planet itself. They consider that research can be used to make positive change as well as contribute to knowledge, and that both are important. They are also increasingly using co-production approaches to research to empower the groups being researched.

Publications Take part in research

Research

  • Older autistic people

    Older autistic people’s experiences of living situations, employment and technology use, and technology development to support increased autonomy and improved quality of life.

  • Mobility for blind people

    Route learning, spatial understanding and representations, and new travel technologies.

  • Accessible learning technologies

    Designing accessible learning technologies for disabled learners.

  • Engineering ethics

    Opening up engineering to minority groups, and the experiences of disabled and LGBTQ+ engineers. Marion convenes the International Federation of Automatic Control (IFAC) Working Group on Ethics.

New article

Older Autistic Adults’ Experiences, Attitudes and Support Needs

New empirical data on the living and employment situations, aspirations and barriers of 78 autistic people aged 50 to 79. The study was carried out by a team of older autistic people, with an advisory board of older autistic people.
