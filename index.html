New research · 2026

Older Autistic Adults’ Experiences, Attitudes and Support Needs

New data on the living and employment situations, aspirations and barriers of 78 autistic people aged 50 to 79, giving them a voice through their own words. Appropriate support emerges as a key enabler of quality of life, but more often its absence acts as a barrier.

  • 78 participants aged 50–79
  • Co-produced by older autistic researchers
  • UKRI Healthy Ageing Catalyst Award

Research

Four connected areas of work

Each uses technology and design to widen what people can do, on their own terms.

  • 01

    Older autistic people

    Living situations, employment and technology use, and technology development to support increased autonomy and quality of life.

  • 02

    Mobility for blind people

    Route learning, spatial understanding and representations, and new travel technologies, including wearable and robotic aids.

  • 03

    Accessible learning technologies

    Designing learning technologies that include disabled learners, from evaluation frameworks to serious games.

  • 04

    Engineering ethics

    Opening up engineering to minority groups, and the experiences of disabled and LGBTQ+ engineers. Convenor of the IFAC Working Group on Ethics.
